Your plan to test day

The DLPT measures reading + listening (multiple-choice, ILR-scored); the OPI is a live speaking interview. Grammar drilling is the foundation — but the tests reward volume of input and hours of talking. Shift your weight across these phases. Confirm your exact level targets with your teacher.

26+ weeks out · Foundation

Lock the engine

Own the present tense for every verb group, plus futur proche, reflexives, negation, possessives, and question forms. Use the Verbes + Grammaire tabs daily (15–20 min). Start gentle listening: slow-French podcasts, the textbook audio. Goal: zero hesitation on Unit 1–2 mechanics.

Verbes daily · Grammaire by chapter · 10 min easy listening
13–26 weeks out · Volume

Flood the input, start producing

Comprehension is built by hours, not rules. Read and listen to authentic material every day (news clips, short articles). Add passé composé + imparfait. Begin speaking aloud daily: describe your routine, give directions, introduce people — the exact OPI task types. Record yourself.

Daily authentic reading + listening · daily 5-min monologue
5–13 weeks out · Conditioning

Train under test conditions

Timed DLPT-style passages weekly — practice reading for gist and detail against the clock. Weekly mock OPI with a partner or tutor: warm-up, level checks, a role-play, wind-down. Feed your "still learning" verb list and missed drills back in until they're clean.

Weekly timed reading/listening · weekly mock OPI
Final 5 weeks · Taper

Sharpen, then rest

One or two full-length timed run-throughs, light review of weak spots, and confirm test logistics. Then ease off — sleep and a calm head do more in the final days than cramming. Confirm your exact test dates and logistics with your teacher.

2 full mocks · light review · rest
